CCCIs rarely live on user fees alone. Most blend **user fees, grants, donations, in-kind contributions, and volunteer labour** — and increasingly borrow or take investment on top. That mix is a strength, but it makes financial discipline non-negotiable: poor money management causes cash-flow crises, donor distrust, missed investment opportunities, and regulatory trouble. As initiatives move toward blended finance, the ability to report assets, investments, income, and expenses accurately is absolutely essential ([Self-sustaining Financing Solutions for Community Connectivity — DC3](https://comconnectivity.org/self-sustaining-financing-solutions-for-community-connectivity/)).

Software is no substitute for accounting expertise — but the right platforms make the job possible for volunteer-run organisations, and produce the transparent reports that funders and communities both demand.

## Blended finance: the funding stack of a CCCI

| Financing model | How it works | Where it fits |
|-----------------|--------------|---------------|
| Blended capital pools | Grants, loans, and equity layered together, often coordinated by an intermediary | Portfolios of small deployments; used by Rhizomatica (Mexico), Zenzeleni (South Africa) |
| Bridge-to-permitting debt | Short-term loans covering the gap between approval and build-out | Proven operators stuck in slow licensing — common in Africa and Latin America |
| TA-linked capital | Funding tied to technical assistance for governance and financial modelling | Early-stage CCCIs moving from donor pilots to revenue models |
| Revolving funds | Repaid loans recycle into new deployments (e.g. CPE lending) | Hardware affordability in rural Africa and South Asia |
| Microfinance partnerships | MFIs finance user hardware in instalments | TandaNET (Kenya) |
| Cross-subsidisation | Business and higher-income users subsidise low-income access | PamojaNet (DRC) — businesses pay daytime, community access is free after hours |
| In-kind and barter | Labour, goods, or produce exchanged for connectivity | Cash-poor rural communities such as Pathardi (India) |

Two rules of thumb from the research: diversify income so no single funder's exit breaks you, and package your network as **social infrastructure** — climate, gender, education, and digital-rights capital flows are increasingly open to community connectivity ([DC3 financing report](https://comconnectivity.org/self-sustaining-financing-solutions-for-community-connectivity/)).

## Bookkeeping and accounting

| Tool | Best at | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| Wave | Free cloud accounting for small organisations | Easy, multi-currency; needs internet |
| GnuCash | Desktop double-entry bookkeeping | Works fully offline on low-end hardware — a real advantage in rural settings |
| Akaunting | Self-hosted accounting and invoicing | Multi-currency; pairs with the Enabling Payments stack |
| ERPNext | Full ERP: accounting, inventory, HR, projects | Self-hosted and highly customisable; needs technical capacity |
| Odoo | Modular ERP/CRM | Powerful but heavier to set up and train on |
| Dolibarr | Lightweight ERP/CRM | A good middle ground for small teams |
| QuickBooks Online | Commercial cloud accounting | Best third-party integration; subscription cost and connectivity required |

**Practices:**

- Open a **dedicated bank account** for the initiative — never mix personal and organisational funds.
- Keep books in **local currency** and foreign grants in a parallel currency ledger; multi-currency tools above help.
- Use **offline-capable tools** (GnuCash) or self-hosted instances where connectivity is unreliable.
- Recruit a treasurer early, provide basic training for volunteers, and standardise invoice and receipt templates.
- Where affordable, engage a local accountant — many work pro bono for community organisations (e.g. via [AfID](https://www.afid.org.uk/)).

## Budgeting for irregular income

- **Rolling forecasts** — re-forecast monthly or quarterly, because grant delays and seasonal payments will break an annual plan.
- **Reserve fund** — build toward 3–6 months of operating expenses before scaling.
- **Zero-based / participatory budgeting** — justify each line item with the community; it aligns spending with community priorities and builds trust.
- **Cost allocation** — tag costs to projects, funders, and user groups so you can show any donor exactly what their money funded.
- **Scenario planning** — model best-, worst-, and likely-case income before taking on debt or payroll.

## Reporting: for donors, regulators, and the community

| Report | Audience | Cadence |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Income and expense statement | Board, funders, community | Monthly or quarterly |
| Balance sheet (assets, liabilities, equity) | Funders, lenders | Quarterly or annual |
| Cash flow statement | Management | Monthly — critical with irregular income |
| Grant reports | Donors, using their templates; track restricted vs. unrestricted funds | As required |
| Tax filings (income tax, VAT where applicable) | Regulators | Annual or per local thresholds |
| Community audit — an open meeting where members review the books | The community | Annual — a hallmark of well-governed CCCIs and cooperatives |

## Funding sources worth knowing

![A community savings group meeting](img/community-finance.jpg)

- **[Internet Society grants](https://www.internetsociety.org/grants/)** and [chapter programmes](https://www.internetsociety.org/) — community networks and digital inclusion
- **[APC subgrants](https://www.apc.org/)** — community networks with a rights and social-justice focus
- **Mozilla / [MOSS](https://foundation.mozilla.org/)** — open-source components and advocacy
- **[Universal Service Funds](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-D/Finance/Pages/USF.aspx)** — most countries have one; some (Argentina, Kenya) are opening them to community initiatives
- **Impact investors and DFIs** — patient capital for proven models (Rhizomatica, Zenzeleni have both gone this route)
- **Crowdfunding** — [Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/) is designed for transparent, values-aligned collective finance (used by guifi.net)
- **In-kind contributions** — donated hardware, bandwidth, venues, and volunteer time; track them in your books even though no cash moves

## Case studies in brief

- **Zenzeleni (South Africa)** — voucher sales + grants + donations; participatory budgeting; bookkeeping in spreadsheets with a migration to Odoo planned. Lesson: diversified revenue plus community involvement. [zenzeleni.net](https://www.zenzeleni.net/)
- **TandaNET (Kenya)** — low-cost subscriptions with donor and capacity-building funding; rolling forecasts; microfinance partnerships for hardware. Lesson: blended finance sustains operations through growth. [tandanet.or.ke](https://tandanet.or.ke/)
- **Pathardi (India)** — annual Gram Panchayat grants + user fees; zero-based budgeting with community input. Lesson: government partnership can anchor revenue. [baif.org.in](https://www.baif.org.in/)
- **Kasepuhan Ciptagelar (Indonesia)** — voucher sales and in-kind contributions; participatory budgeting with indigenous leaders. Lesson: barter and in-kind models work in cash-poor communities. [commonroom.info](https://commonroom.info/)
- **PamojaNet (DRC)** — daytime business fees fund free after-hours community access; spreadsheet books with published reporting. Lesson: cross-subsidisation makes access affordable for all. [pamojanet.cd](https://pamojanet.cd/)

All five are documented in depth in [Self-sustaining Financing Solutions for Community Connectivity](https://comconnectivity.org/self-sustaining-financing-solutions-for-community-connectivity/).

## Managing money in Global Majority contexts

- **Currency volatility** — keep reserves in hard currency or stablecoins where legal; prefer funders who disburse in local currency; use multi-currency tools to see real costs.
- **Cash-heavy operations** — reconcile agent collections on a fixed weekly rhythm; keep cash floats small and insured.
- **Limited banking access** — lean on mobile money and agent networks for both collection and payment of suppliers.
- **Regulatory constraints on funding** — register properly (see [Regulatory Compliance](regulatory-compliance.html)) so you can legally receive foreign grants; document everything.
- **Financial literacy gaps** — run workshops and pair the treasurer with a mentor; simple, visual reports beat complex ones.

## Suggested roadmap

| Stage | Focus |
|-------|-------|
| **Start (0–3 months)** | Dedicated bank account; spreadsheet or GnuCash books; separate ledgers per funding source; publish monthly numbers to the community |
| **Grow (3–12 months)** | Move to Wave or Akaunting; rolling forecasts; 3-month reserve target; cost tagging per funder/project |
| **Professionalise (12+ months)** | ERPNext/Odoo with automation; annual community audit; investor-grade reporting; impact metrics and audited statements for blended finance |
